Urbex Simulator

Urbex Simulator is a first-person multiplayer game about exploring abandoned places with friends. Bring a camera, flashlight, spray paint, and glowsticks, pick a map in the lobby, and see how far you get before the building, the layout, or the fall damage stops you.

How It Plays

There is no strict win condition right now. You drop in, explore at your own pace, take photos, tag walls, call out hazards, and push the map as far as you can before somebody makes a bad decision.

Movement

Walk, sprint, jump, and climb short ledges or climbable surfaces. Sprint drains stamina, so save it for long runs or bad landings.

Inventory

Carry your camera, flashlight, spray paint, paintball gun, and glowstick bundle through a 5-slot hotbar. Pick things up with E and drop them with Q.

Multiplayer

Host a lobby, pick a map together, and explore as a group. Everyone keeps their own photo roll, and the lobby shows you a preview before the run starts.

Tools You Carry

Camera Press C to bring up the viewfinder, zoom with the scroll wheel, and take photos of the places you want to remember.
Flashlight A physical flashlight you can pick up and drop. Its wide beam stays useful even if your FOV changes, and some lights have limited battery.
Spray paint Tag walls, mark routes, or leave messages for your crew. Adjust the spray size with the scroll wheel and pick your color with P.
Paintball gun Shoot paint from a distance when you want to tag hard-to-reach spots or start trouble from across the room.
Glowsticks Drop or place glowsticks to light the way, mark danger, or keep your bearings when a flashlight is not enough.
Voice chat Push-to-talk is on V by default, so you can keep the cross-talk manageable while everyone is getting spooked.

What's Coming

The game is still in active development, so expect rough edges and things changing between updates. The long-term goal is to give players more places to explore, more ways to get into trouble, and more reasons to keep coming back.

  • More maps and locations with different moods and layouts.
  • Hazards like rotten structures, flooding, power cycling, and other places that actively fight back.
  • More game modes beyond free-roam exploration, including paintball and hide-and-seek style play.
  • Customization for clothing, hold styles, visual effects, and noise effects.
  • A proper tutorial / onboarding flow so new players can learn without getting yelled at too hard.
  • More items, objectives, and exploration tools for longer sessions with friends.
